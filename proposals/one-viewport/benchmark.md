## Decisions carried into this proposal

**Clarity before cleverness:** one benefit-led headline, a category/platform eyebrow and a three-sentence subhead. An original recipe shows routine → tiny action → celebration. The headline does not have to teach all of habit science.

**One action, not one long journey:** the Windows preview CTA is visible without scrolling at both target desktop viewports. No top-level feature/pricing/login maze. Current owner-test restrictions stay beside it; a clear dialog leads to existing release help, not an automatic installer. The preview is not yet a general-customer product.

**Use product truth as credibility:** a labeled interface concept and concrete example, not invented testimonials, stars, users, badges or “proven app” copy. Research links are a quiet first-screen cue and optional below-fold teaching, not a substitute for product validation.

**Borrow hierarchy, not a brand:** generous whitespace, sharp type, one accent and a single illustrative surface. No copied screenshots, graphics, testimonials or claims from these companies. No animation is needed to convey the idea.

| [Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010), DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674), *European Journal of Social Psychology*, 40(6), 998–1009 | Real-world repetition in consistent context and growing self-reported automaticity; modeled time to 95% asymptote varied 18–254 days. It was a 12-week study, so modeled times can extend beyond observation. | A universal deadline, “21 days,” a guarantee based on a 66-day median, causal proof for Tiny Habits/celebration, or any test of Bloomstep. |

The public index corroborated the science statements and DOI; a fresh original publisher PDF could not be retrieved here. The proposal uses conservative attribution and original examples, not book excerpts. Before any production launch, recheck source destinations and the owner-approved final wording. App installation readiness and privacy requirements remain separate blockers.
